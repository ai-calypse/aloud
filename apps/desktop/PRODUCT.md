# Product

## Register

product

## Users

Mac power users (developers and makers) who spend the day inside other apps: an editor, a terminal, a browser. They talk to Aloud to dictate and to run commands, and they glance at the notch for a second at a time: what's playing, whether an agent needs them, how long is left on a timer, what just got saved. Then they go back to their work. The notch must never pull them out of what they were doing longer than the glance itself.

## Product Purpose

Aloud turns speech into text and actions on the Mac. Hold a key and clean text lands in the focused app, or say a command and an agent does it across Mac apps and connected services. The notch is where all of it surfaces: the recording state, the agent's steps and approval cards, confirmations, and a utility layer of glanceable tools (media, focus, files, clipboard, system status) in the spirit of a Dynamic Island.

Success is a user who stops noticing the notch is there until it has something worth a glance, and who can reach any tool in it in one move.

## Brand Personality

Calm, precise, native. It should feel like Apple shipped it with macOS: Control Center, Now Playing and the Dynamic Island are the references. Quiet until needed, then exact. Motion that feels physical, emerging from the notch rather than appearing on top of the screen. Confident, never chatty.

## Anti-references

- **Gimmicky glass.** Heavy blur and glow on everything, neon accents, glass used as decoration rather than as the one material that separates the notch from the desktop.
- **A crowded icon dock.** Twenty unlabelled icons along an edge that you have to memorise (Notchy's bottom bar). Every destination is named or reachable by search.
- Web-dashboard habits: hero metrics with gradient accents, cards nested in cards, admin-panel chrome.

## Design Principles

1. **A glance, not a visit.** Every surface answers its question in under a second. The most important fact leads; detail is one step away, never in the way.
2. **Emerge from the notch.** Things grow out of where they come from and settle back into it. Nothing pops into existence over the user's work.
3. **Named, not memorised.** Destinations carry words. Icons support labels; they never replace them. Search and the keyboard reach everything.
4. **Native first.** Match what macOS would do: system typography, system colours, system motion. A web idiom is a bug.
5. **Quiet until it matters.** Live activity earns space by what it costs to miss. Everything else waits to be asked.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

- WCAG 2.2 AA contrast for all text on the dark floating surface, including secondary and placeholder text.
- Reduced motion is a hard switch: travel collapses, crossfades remain, and nothing animates its way into view only on a class change.
- Every action is reachable by keyboard (tab order, ⌘1–9 section switching, Esc to close), with visible focus.
- Status is never colour-only: a red recording state or a strict card also carries a word or a shape.
